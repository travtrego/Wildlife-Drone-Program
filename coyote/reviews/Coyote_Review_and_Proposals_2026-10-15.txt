1. VERDICT ON THE CURRENT REPORT
--------------------------------
What's good:
- Honest about evidence. Separates CANDIDATE / ESTIMATE / OPEN items.
- Doesn't trust the E616P "16 kg effective load" marketing number.
- Keeps flight-critical safety on Pixhawk/PX4, not the Jetson.
- Arithmetic checks out: 23.78 kg model AUW, ~19.9 idealized min,
  ~53 A hover on the 22Ah case all reproduce.

Core problem:
The three requirements conflict. A full sensor suite, a 25–30 min
endurance target, and an AUW under 55 lb can't all be met by the current
design. The report's own numbers show this; it should state it plainly.

Specific issues:
- The 22Ah light pack is effectively disqualified. ~53–61 A hover against
  a 66 A continuous rating leaves no margin for climb, gusts or spikes.
- The 80% usable-energy figure has no landing reserve and no
  wind/maneuver margin. Real mission time will be well below the
  idealized numbers.
- The 1.17 kg margin under 55 lb is likely already gone. Mounts, landing
  gear and enclosure (1.2 kg) and harness/power distribution (0.65 kg)
  look low for a 3 kg gimbal, a LiDAR mast and 12S wiring at ~100 A
  peaks. Nothing is budgeted for a second battery.
- The T7 + ILX-LR1 + 70–200 package (~3.3 kg with collar/zoom hardware)
  is the single biggest avoidable weight. The lens has a mechanical zoom
  ring, so in practice it would be flown at a fixed focal length anyway.


2. PROPOSALS
------------

P1. SPLIT THE CAMERA JOB  [OWNER DECISION REQUIRED]
Replace T7 + SEL70200G with two permanently installed units:
  a) Sony ILX-LR1 + FE 85mm F1.8 on a Gremsy Pixy LR (~1.1 kg package,
     prior screening). Gremsy lists the 85mm as compatible but
     "photography only"; that's fine because video comes from (b).
     Role: 61MP color stills for ID, mapping, high-res records.
  b) Integrated zoom/thermal gimbal, e.g. SIYI ZT30 (870 g vendor spec):
     30x optical zoom, 4K30 video, 640x512 thermal, laser rangefinder
     (5–1200 m), 3-axis, MAVLink/PX4, Ethernet video.
     Role: zoomed video, thermal detection, range-to-animal logging.
Net effect: ~1.3 kg saved. Real in-flight zoom without motorizing a
mechanical zoom ring. The rangefinder logs stand-off distance.
Still satisfies "no sensor swaps": all hardware is permanently installed.
Tradeoffs / open items:
  - The telephoto moves off the Sony, which changes the current owner
    requirement wording.
  - The ZT30's thermal may not match a Boson+ for research-grade or
    radiometric data (UNVERIFIED). Keep the Boson+ as an option if needed.
  - ZT30 4K is small-sensor video: fine for detection and ID frames,
    not documentary grade (owner confirmed documentary grade not needed).
  - If Sony video is ever wanted, use a 55mm on the Pixy LR instead of
    the 85mm (not flagged photo-only by Gremsy).
Ruled out: Gremsy T3V3 (1.2 kg, 1.7 kg payload). Its 100 mm cage depth is
too short for the 70–200.

P2. KEEP THE HOBBYWING X8 (CORRECTS AN EARLIER SUGGESTION)
I first suggested lighter motors. That was wrong. Per the vendor tables:
  - X6 Plus saves ~1.8 kg total but needs ~449 W vs ~380 W per motor at
    ~4 kg thrust (24" vs 30" props), so the net endurance is worse. Its
    30 mm arm also doesn't match the E616P's ~40 mm.
  - The X8 G2 weighs 1,095 g (not 1,150 g as modeled): -0.33 kg total.
  - At ~4 kg/axis you're below the X8 G2's recommended 5–7.5 kg/axis band.
    That's fine for efficiency and gives good motor-out margin (~4.9
    kg/axis on 5 motors vs 17.5 kg max thrust).
  - Confirm the X8 G2 clamp fits the exact E616P arm diameter.

P3. PICK THE BATTERY BY CURRENT FIRST, CAPACITY SECOND
  - Require continuous discharge >= ~2x hover current (~130 A).
  - The 22Ah light pack (66 A) fails. Check C-rating, BMS, connector,
    fusing, bay fit and CG for the 33Ah-class options.

P4. HONEST ENDURANCE MODEL AND TARGET
Revised assumptions (Claude estimate):
  - X8 G2 vendor 46V thrust/power table, interpolated
  - 150 W non-propulsion load (was 120 W)
  - 70% usable energy, i.e. land with ~25% reserve (was 80%, no reserve)
  - +10% wind/maneuver margin
  - Mounts +0.4 kg, harness +0.25 kg over the original placeholders

  Config            Battery  AUW (lb)        Realistic min
  ----------------  -------  --------------  -------------
  Current design    22Ah     53.1            ~15
  Current design    26Ah     54.8            ~17
  Current design    33Ah     56.9 (OVER 55)  ~21
  Current design    36Ah     57.7 (OVER 55)  ~22
  Split-RGB (P1)    22Ah     50.1            ~16
  Split-RGB (P1)    26Ah     51.8            ~19
  Split-RGB (P1)    33Ah     53.9            ~22   <- recommended
  Split-RGB (P1)    36Ah     54.7 (too thin) ~24

Recommendation: Split-RGB + 33Ah-class pack, ~22 min, ~1 lb margin under 55.
Change the acceptance target to ">= 20 min usable with 25% reserve,
measured in hover logs". Treat 25–30 min as a stretch goal.
Formulas:
  per_rotor_kg = AUW / 6
  watts = (6 * X8G2_power(per_rotor_kg) + 150) * 1.10
  minutes = (Ah * 44.4 * 0.70 / watts) * 60

3. MISSION FOCUS: IDENTIFYING INDIVIDUAL COYOTES
------------------------------------------------
Owner goal: identify individual coyotes. Documentary-grade video is not
needed.

Reality check: coyotes are a "plain" species with no unique coat pattern,
so ID is hard regardless of camera. The closest evidence is a red fox
camera-trap study: one trained observer identified individuals in ~99% of
~170k photos (192 foxes), using:
  - Short fur color on the muzzle, chest and legs
  - Tail shape, ear shape and notches, scars, injuries, deformities
  - Body size, build and condition
  - Many high-res color photos from multiple angles
  - Location and time context to narrow candidates
  - A human doing the matching. The authors doubted automated systems
    would reach that accuracy.
A separate study found that ID errors systematically inflate population
estimates, so mistakes matter.

Implications for Coyote:
  - Angle matters more than zoom. Top-down views hide the muzzle, chest
    and legs. Fly oblique/side-on from stand-off distance.
  - Thermal detects and locates but won't identify. ID needs daylight
    color imagery.
  - The split setup fits this well: ZT30 30fps zoom video gives many
    angles per pass, and the Sony at 85mm gives 61MP stills.
  - RTK geotags + timestamps supply the territory context that made the
    fox study work.
  - Workflow: the Jetson detects and tracks; a human confirms IDs. Don't
    plan on fully automated ID.

Acceptance test BEFORE major purchases:
  Place a coyote-size target with small ear-notch and scar markings at
  50, 100 and 150 m. Check whether the ZT30 at max zoom and the Sony at
  85mm resolve them. The result sets the real stand-off distance. That
  matters because a 30-inch hexacopter is loud and coyotes are wary.

Research coordination:
  - Talk to RI DEM early about permits and methods.
  - Ear tags or collars on a few animals (by authorized researchers) give
    ground truth to check your IDs against.
